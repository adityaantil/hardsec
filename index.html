// The idea

A chip never tells you its secret. But it hums while it works.

In its power draw, in its timing, in what happens when you nudge it. HardSec 2026 is where you learn to listen, to push, and to prove that "secure" hardware is a claim you can test. You do not need to be an expert. You need to be curious enough to ask "what if I push here?"

  • Timing
  • Power
  • Memory
  • Faults

// Who can join

If you can write a little code, you belong here.

Know any one of these? You are in.

PythonorCorVerilog

Basic is enough. A loop, an if-statement, one small Verilog module. Day 1 teaches the security part from scratch, so you do not need any hardware-security background.

First-year BTech students are warmly welcome. So are MTech and PhD scholars who want to try attacking real hardware.

What you need

  • Engineering student, any branch, any year

  • No hardware to buy, no prior security experience
  • Come solo or with a team; we help you find teammates on Day 1
  • On-site at IIIT Allahabad, or remote from anywhere in India
// Two-day operation

Learn on Day 1. Break the cipher on Day 2.

DAY 1

Learn to read the leak

  • Memory-array vulnerabilities and real attack surfaces
  • Side-channel leakage, from first principles to correlation
  • Fault injection: how one glitch becomes a bypass
  • Guided, hands-on practice that prepares you for every track on Day 2
DAY 2

Break the cipher

  • The hackathon runs in three phases: discover, engineer, invent
  • Each phase is a track with its own challenge, tools and scoring
  • On-site at IIIT Allahabad or remote, on the same leaderboard
  • Prizes from a ₹30,000+ pool and certificates for every participant
// The hackathon

Three phases. Three tracks.

The hackathon moves through three phases. First you find the weakness, then you build the defense, and finally you bring an idea of your own.

  1. Phase 1Track A
    A

    Discover the vulnerability

    Identify an access-control failure, data corruption, predictable access pattern, or information leakage in a provided memory model. Use Python or C to analyze access logs and demonstrate the weakness.

    • Python
    • C
    • Access-log analysis
  2. Phase 2Track B
    B

    Engineer the defense

    Implement a secure memory wrapper, access monitor, address checker, integrity mechanism, or another protection technique using Verilog/SystemVerilog and Vivado.

    • Verilog
    • SystemVerilog
    • Vivado
  3. Phase 3Track C
    C

    Invent your own solution

    Propose a different attack or defense, compare alternative architectures, or build an automated vulnerability detector. Novel approaches are welcome if the team can explain and validate the result.

    • Your choice of tools
    • Open-ended
Teams are judged onTechnical depthA working demonstrationA clear explanationValidation of the result

No expert needed. Day 1 prepares you for every track, so a first-year with basic code and a sharp mind has a fair way in, and a final-year has plenty to chew on.

// Why it matters

This is a weekend that can move your career.

Software skills are easy to find in a resume pile. Someone who has attacked real hardware is not.

01 //

A skill few graduates have

Hardware security sits between electronics and cybersecurity. Few students leave college having attacked a real chip, and that gap shows up in interviews.

02 //

Proof for your resume

Leave with a write-up of the flaw you found, the demo that proved it and the defense you built. Put it on GitHub and in your CV.

03 //

Real attacker thinking

Side-channel and fault attacks are studied in research labs and security teams around the world. You get a first taste of how they think.

04 //

A door into research

Memory security, side-channel and fault attacks are active research areas at IEEE venues. A good Day 2 can grow into a project, a paper or a thesis idea.

05 //

Roles it points toward

Chip and embedded security, secure firmware, hardware verification, VLSI and semiconductor design, and security research.

06 //

People and credibility

A certificate from an IEEE CASS-supported event, plus mentors, faculty and sharp peers from across India.

Anyone can use a secure chip. The people who learn to break one get to build the next. Start with one track. See where it takes you.

// Before you ask

Frequently asked questions.

I am in 1st year and only know basic coding. Can I join?

Yes. Knowing basic Python, C or Verilog (any one) is enough. Day 1 teaches the security ideas from the ground up, and every track gives first-time participants a fair way in.

What do I need to bring?

A laptop. Track B uses Vivado for Verilog/SystemVerilog work, and we share the setup steps after you register. You do not need to buy any hardware.

What are the three phases?

Track A: discover a vulnerability in a provided memory model. Track B: engineer a defense in Verilog/SystemVerilog. Track C: invent your own attack, defense or detector. Day 1 prepares you for all three.

Can I take part remotely?

Yes. Remote teams compete on the same leaderboard and for the same prizes as the on-site teams.

Solo or team?

Either works. Come with a team, or come solo and we will help you find teammates at the start of Day 1.

How are teams judged?

On technical depth, a working demonstration, how clearly you explain it, and whether you can validate your result.

Is it free?

Free for IEEE/CASS student members. Everyone else pays a nominal ₹549 as registration fee.

Do I get a certificate?

Yes. Every participant gets a certificate, and the top teams share prizes from the ₹30,000+ pool.

When does registration open?

Soon. The event dates are tentative until registration opens. Email us and we will tell you the moment it does.
